# Investment Analysis Framework

## Valuation Methods

### Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
- A P/E below 15 is generally considered undervalued for mature companies.
- Growth stocks often trade at P/E ratios of 25-50x or higher.
- Compare P/E to the sector average and the company's historical average.
- Forward P/E is more useful than trailing P/E for growth companies.

### EV/EBITDA
- EV/EBITDA below 10x is generally considered attractive.
- This metric is better than P/E for comparing companies with different capital structures.
- Useful for comparing companies across borders with different tax regimes.

### Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)
- Project Free Cash Flow for 5-10 years using conservative growth assumptions.
- Use WACC (Weighted Average Cost of Capital) as the discount rate, typically 8-12%.
- Terminal growth rate should not exceed long-term GDP growth (2-3%).
- Sensitivity analysis: vary growth rate ±2% and discount rate ±1% to create a valuation range.
- A margin of safety of 20-30% below intrinsic value provides downside protection.

### Price-to-Book (P/B) Ratio
- P/B below 1.0 may indicate undervaluation, but verify asset quality.
- Financial companies are best valued using P/B ratios.
- Technology companies often have high P/B due to intangible assets.

## Financial Health Indicators

### Profitability
- Gross margin > 40% indicates strong pricing power.
- Operating margin > 20% suggests operational efficiency.
- Net margin > 10% is generally healthy for most industries.
- ROE > 15% indicates efficient use of shareholder equity.
- ROA > 5% shows efficient asset utilization.

### Leverage
- Debt-to-Equity below 1.0 is conservative.
- Interest coverage ratio > 5x provides comfortable debt servicing.
- Current ratio > 1.5 indicates adequate short-term liquidity.
- Quick ratio > 1.0 ensures ability to meet immediate obligations.

### Cash Flow
- Operating cash flow should exceed net income (high earnings quality).
- Free cash flow yield > 5% is attractive for value investors.
- Consistent positive free cash flow over 5+ years indicates business stability.

## Sentiment Analysis Framework

### Bullish Indicators
- Insider buying activity (especially by CEO/CFO).
- Analyst upgrades and increasing price targets.
- Positive earnings surprises for 3+ consecutive quarters.
- Increasing institutional ownership.
- Strong consumer sentiment in surveys.

### Bearish Indicators
- Insider selling (especially large blocks).
- Analyst downgrades and lowered price targets.
- Negative earnings surprises or lowered guidance.
- Decreasing institutional ownership.
- Short interest above 10% of float.

## Investment Report Best Practices

### Bull Case Construction
- Identify 3-5 specific catalysts that could drive upside.
- Quantify the potential upside (target price, expected return).
- Assess probability and timeline for each catalyst.

### Bear Case Construction
- Identify 3-5 specific risks that could cause underperformance.
- Quantify potential downside scenarios.
- Consider both company-specific and macro risks.

### Recommendation Framework
- BUY: Expected return > 15% over 12 months with favorable risk/reward.
- HOLD: Expected return 0-15% or uncertain near-term catalysts.
- SELL: Expected negative return or deteriorating fundamentals.
