# Peter Lynch's Stock Classification — Applied to Indian Markets

## Six Categories of Stocks

### 1. Slow Growers (Stalwarts)
Revenue growth: 2-5% | Dividend yield: High | P/E: Low-Moderate

**Indian examples:**
- Coal India (government monopoly, limited growth, high dividend)
- Power Grid Corporation (regulated returns, steady income)
- NTPC (capacity additions slow, focuses on dividends)
- ITC (cigarette business is cash cow, limited volume growth)

**Investment approach:** Buy for dividends, moderate expectations.

### 2. Stalwarts (Steady Eddies)
Revenue growth: 8-12% | Consistent earnings | P/E: 15-25x

**Indian examples:**
- Hindustan Unilever (India's FMCG leader, steady 10-12% growth)
- TCS (predictable IT services revenue, strong cash generation)
- HDFC Bank (consistent 15-18% loan growth, stable NIMs)
- Nestle India (premium food segment, pricing power)

**Investment approach:** Core portfolio holdings. Buy on dips, sell at 30-40% gains.

### 3. Fast Growers
Revenue growth: >20% | High P/E | High reinvestment

**Indian examples:**
- Bajaj Finance (30%+ AUM growth in consumer lending)
- Dmart (Avenue Supermarts — rapid store expansion)
- Trent (Zudio/Westside — fast-growing fashion retail)
- Dixon Technologies (electronics manufacturing, PLI beneficiary)

**Investment approach:** Highest returns potential. Watch for growth deceleration.

### 4. Cyclicals
Earnings tied to economic cycles | Buy when P/E is HIGH (near trough)

**Indian examples:**
- Tata Steel, JSW Steel (steel cycle)
- Hindalco (aluminium/copper cycle)
- DLF, Godrej Properties (real estate cycle)
- SBI, Bank of Baroda (banking credit cycle)
- Ashok Leyland (commercial vehicle cycle)

**Investment approach:** Buy when P/E is optically high (earnings are depressed), sell when P/E looks low (peak earnings).

### 5. Turnarounds
Distressed companies recovering | High risk, high reward

**Indian examples (historical):**
- Tata Motors (JLR turnaround + EV transition)
- YES Bank (post-RBI intervention recovery)
- Vodafone Idea (potential tariff-driven recovery)
- IDBI Bank (post-LIC acquisition restructuring)

**Investment approach:** Only invest what you can afford to lose. Look for debt reduction and management change.

### 6. Asset Plays
Market value < asset value | Hidden assets not reflected in stock price

**Indian examples:**
- ITC (hotel properties, agri business undervalued vs cigarette earnings)
- NMDC (iron ore reserves valued below market)
- Land bank companies (DLF, Prestige — land values exceed market cap components)

**Investment approach:** Patience required. Wait for catalysts to unlock value.

## Lynch's Key Ratios for Indian Stocks
- **PEG Ratio**: P/E divided by earnings growth rate. PEG < 1 is attractive.
- **Debt-Free Investing**: Lynch preferred companies with minimal debt.
- **Insider Buying**: In Indian context = promoter buying from open market (very bullish signal)
