# Nifty 50 Sector Analysis

## IT Services (Weight: ~14%)
**Key stocks:** TCS, Infosys, Wipro, HCL Technologies, Tech Mahindra

**Business model:** Indian IT companies earn 70-90% revenue from exports, primarily to US and Europe. Revenue is predominantly in USD, creating a natural hedge against rupee depreciation.

**Key metrics to watch:**
- Revenue growth in constant currency
- EBIT margins (industry average: 20-25%)
- Attrition rate (impacts margins and delivery quality)
- Deal wins (TCV — Total Contract Value)
- Fresher hiring as a lead indicator of demand

**Valuation:** P/E 20-30x for large caps. ROCE typically 30-50% (asset-light model). High dividend payout. Minimal debt.

**Risks:** US recession, technology disruption (GenAI impact on services), visa restrictions, client concentration.

## Banking & Financial Services (Weight: ~33%)
**Key stocks:** HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Kotak Mahindra, SBI, Axis Bank, Bajaj Finance

**Key metrics:**
- NIM (Net Interest Margin): Private banks 3.5-4.5%, PSU banks 2.5-3.5%
- GNPA (Gross Non-Performing Assets): <2% is excellent, >5% is concerning
- CASA ratio: Higher is better (cheaper funding)
- Credit growth: 12-15% is healthy for India
- ROA: >1.5% is excellent for banks
- Provision coverage ratio: >70% is healthy

**Valuation:** P/B ratio is preferred for banks. Top private banks: 2.5-4.0x P/B. PSU banks: 0.8-1.5x P/B.

**Risks:** Asset quality deterioration, regulatory changes (RBI), rising credit costs.

## Energy (Weight: ~12%)
**Key stocks:** Reliance Industries, ONGC, BPCL, IOC, NTPC, Power Grid

**Reliance Industries** is India's most valuable company — a conglomerate spanning:
- Oil refining (world's largest refinery complex at Jamnagar)
- Telecom (Jio — 450M+ subscribers)
- Retail (Reliance Retail — India's largest retailer)
- New energy (solar, hydrogen, battery)

**Key metrics for energy:** Refining margins (GRM), crude oil prices, gas realization, capacity utilization.

**Valuation:** SOTP (Sum of Parts) is preferred for Reliance. Oil PSUs trade at low P/E (8-12x) due to government intervention risk.

## FMCG / Consumer Staples (Weight: ~9%)
**Key stocks:** Hindustan Unilever, ITC, Nestle India, Britannia, Dabur

**Key metrics:**
- Volume growth (more important than value growth)
- Rural vs urban demand split
- Market share trends
- Innovation pipeline and new product launches
- Distribution reach (HUL reaches 9M+ outlets)

**Valuation:** Premium P/E (40-70x) due to brand moats, consistent cash flows, and limited capex needs. ROCE typically >50%.

**Risks:** Input cost inflation (palm oil, crude derivatives), rural demand slowdown, competitive intensity from D2C brands.

## Automobiles (Weight: ~7%)
**Key stocks:** Maruti Suzuki, Tata Motors, Mahindra & Mahindra, Bajaj Auto, Hero MotoCorp, Eicher Motors

**Key metrics:**
- Monthly sales volumes (SIAM data)
- Average Selling Price (ASP) trend
- Market share by segment (PV, CV, 2W)
- EV transition readiness
- Export contribution

**Valuation:** P/E 15-30x. Cyclical — buy when sentiment is poor. Tata Motors trades on JLR + India auto dual thesis.

**Risks:** Semiconductor shortages, EV transition, emission norms, rural demand (for 2-wheelers).

## Pharma & Healthcare (Weight: ~4%)
**Key stocks:** Sun Pharma, Dr Reddy's, Cipla, Divi's Laboratories, Apollo Hospitals

**Key metrics:**
- US vs India revenue split
- ANDA filings and approvals (US generics pipeline)
- R&D spend as % of revenue
- API (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient) vs formulation mix
- USFDA inspection outcomes (critical for US revenue)

**Valuation:** P/E 20-35x. Companies with US exposure trade at premium. API players (Divi's) trade at higher multiples.

**Risks:** US pricing pressure, FDA regulatory actions, raw material price volatility.

## Metals & Mining (Weight: ~3%)
**Key stocks:** Tata Steel, Hindalco, JSW Steel, Coal India, Vedanta

**Key metrics:**
- Steel/aluminium prices (LME benchmarks)
- Cost per tonne
- Capacity utilization
- China demand and production data
- Debt levels (metals companies tend to be leveraged)

**Valuation:** EV/EBITDA preferred over P/E (earnings are highly cyclical). Buy when EV/EBITDA is 4-5x (trough), sell at 8-10x (peak).

**Risks:** Global commodity cycle, China policy, energy costs, environmental regulations.
