# Warren Buffett's Investment Principles — Applied to Indian Markets

## Core Principles

### 1. Economic Moat (Competitive Advantage)
Look for companies with durable competitive advantages that protect profits over decades.

**Indian companies with strong moats:**
- **Brand moat**: Hindustan Unilever (consumer brands), Asian Paints (dealer network), Titan (Tanishq brand)
- **Network effect**: HDFC Bank (distribution network), Bajaj Finance (customer ecosystem)
- **Cost advantage**: TCS/Infosys (talent pool, scale), Coal India (monopoly)
- **Switching costs**: Oracle Financial (FLEXCUBE in banks), Tata Consultancy (enterprise contracts)
- **Government license/monopoly**: Coal India, IRCTC, IEX

### 2. Quality of Management
- Track record of capital allocation (ROE, ROCE consistency over 10+ years)
- Promoter integrity — check for related-party transactions, pledge status
- Dividend history and buyback decisions
- Conservative accounting practices (watch for aggressive revenue recognition)

### 3. Circle of Competence
- Understand the business model before investing
- Indian conglomerates (Reliance, Tata, Adani) require understanding multiple businesses
- Stick to sectors you understand — IT, banking, FMCG are well-documented

### 4. Margin of Safety
- Buy at a discount to intrinsic value
- In Indian markets, quality companies rarely trade at deep discounts
- Use market corrections (COVID crash, global rate hikes) as buying opportunities
- Be patient — Indian markets can stay expensive for extended periods

### 5. Long-Term Holding Period
- "Our favorite holding period is forever"
- Indian wealth creation stories: HDFC Bank (50x in 20 years), TCS (100x since listing)
- Compounding works best with high ROCE companies held for 10+ years
- Avoid frequent trading — STT and taxes erode returns in India

## Financial Health Checklist (Buffett Framework for Indian Stocks)
1. Consistent revenue growth >10% CAGR over 5 years
2. Stable or expanding operating margins
3. ROE > 15% consistently
4. ROCE > 15% consistently (key Indian metric)
5. Debt/Equity < 1.0 (preferably < 0.5)
6. Free Cash Flow positive and growing
7. Interest coverage ratio > 3x
8. Promoter holding > 40% with minimal pledge
9. No frequent equity dilution
10. Dividend track record or consistent buybacks
